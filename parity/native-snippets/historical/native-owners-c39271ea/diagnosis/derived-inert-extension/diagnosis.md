Source-confirmed delayed-work candidate

The native focus-manager's complete 964-line body is byte-identical to the body fully read in the initial packet. Its source `getProps` destructuring (`createFloatingFocusManager.svelte.ts:249-263`) creates owner-bound derived prop readers. `store` (`:265`), `events/dataRef` (`:270`), and `getInsideElements` likewise remain owner-bound. `getNodeId` (`:272`) resolves through that derived dataRef; `getResolvedInsideElements` (`:364-365`) resolves through the derived prop getter.

The focus-listener effect installs handleFocusOutside on real reference/floating nodes (`:595-603`). The handler records DOM-event values, then schedules a native microtask (`:476`). Once queued, removing the listeners cannot cancel it. That callback begins with `getNodeId`, a `store` read, and `getResolvedInsideElements` (`:477-479`), followed by previous/next-focusable-element getters, tree, restoreFocus and modal-related getters. If the component is removed between the focus-out event and the microtask, those reads run outside Svelte's synchronous teardown exemption and their owner is already destroyed. This is a concrete source opportunity regardless of whether it produced the two observed warnings. It is brief delayed work, not proof that the parent effect or tree subscription itself survives.

The Original complete FloatingFocusManager business body was already full-body read and was checked again at the exact relevant closure. Its `:443-555` has the same deferred focus-out decisions and listener-only cleanup (`:571-578`). Original store/context values and useStableCallback locals/latest readers do not have Svelte's destroyed-derived rule. Retaining that React lifetime behavior through native rune getters therefore needs explicit native pending-work lifetime handling.

The smallest business-preserving candidate is a component-local disposed flag set by the native `onDestroy` and checked as the first statement of that queued focus-out callback. This adds no dependency guard or new lifetime root: it cancels pending native work once the component owner has actually ended. Keeping the flag at component-owner scope is narrower than invalidating the callback on every effect rerun, which could skip legitimate Original focus-out decisions merely because live props changed. A listener-installation lifetime flag may also be valid if integration demonstrates the desired behavior, but it has that broader behavior surface.

Keep all real-node, owner-document, open/disabled, focus/tree-membership, restoration, related-focus-guard and close-request business inside the live-owner callback. Do not wrap its reads in untrack; suppress the warning; create $effect.root; keep a React-style commit owner alive; change the generic Store API; cache a tuple or fake committed snapshot; or introduce generic ref custody.

The initial-focus microtask (`:668`) and its future rAF predicate also access live rune-backed prop readers, so they are a separate possible post-owner boundary in the same whole body. Their documented intentional focus semantics require separate review before changing their cancellation. The return-focus teardown resolves the element and option during synchronous teardown and queues a captured-target focus operation (`:832-877`); that intentional after-unmount operation must remain. This diagnosis does not cancel return focus or claim every deferred callback is defective.
